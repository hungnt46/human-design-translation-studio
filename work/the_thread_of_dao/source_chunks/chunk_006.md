<span style=" font-size:1.0rem">     While Legalism argued that strict enforcement of laws would ensure order, the proto-Daoist texts within the</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Guan Zi</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">act as a counterbalance to this position by pointing to deeper sources of social co-operation and harmony: inner peace and inner harmony â€“ the â€œtrue natureâ€? of human beings. The</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Four Canons</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">, perhaps written somewhat earlier than the</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Guan Zi</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">, closer to 400 BC, resembles Lao Ziâ€™s philosophy that rulers must be frugal, modest, and humble. Overall,</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Four Canons</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">is closer to Legalism than the</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Guan Zi</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">.</span>

<span style=" font-size:1.0rem">     Though the</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Four Canons</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">may pre-date the</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Guan Zi</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">as an example of Daoist philosophy, the</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Bai Xin, Xin Shu,</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">and</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Nei Ye</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">not only adhere more closely to Lao Ziâ€™s philosophy, but also to the idioms, form, and style in which his proposals and prescriptions appear in the</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Dao De Jing</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">. <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">     The</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Bai Xin, Xin Shu,</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">and</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem; font-style:italic">Nei Ye</span> <span style=" font-size:1.0rem">, along with all Warring States era Daoist texts, appear to have come from the culture of Chu State, located in the south of China, though they may have come to fruition at Jixia Academy.
